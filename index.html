Warm-up 2: Past simple or past continuous?

A short contrast before we start

We use the past continuous for an action in progress at a specific moment: I was watching TV at 8 p.m. We use the past simple for a complete action or event: I watched a film last night.

A storm at the university 13 min

Reading a scene description

Read the text about a storm at the university. Then do the activities.

A storm at the university

Last Tuesday at 4 p.m., a big storm started at the university. It was raining heavily and the wind was blowing very hard. Let me tell you what was happening inside the building.

In the main hall, a group of students were waiting for the storm to stop. Some of them were playing cards on the floor. Near the entrance, two professors were watching the rain through the window. They were talking about the traffic.

In the library, nobody was leaving. Everybody was reading or was studying quietly, and the librarian was closing the windows. In the cafeteria, the staff were serving hot tea to the students, because many of them were cold.

At 5 p.m., the storm stopped, and the sun was shining again. Everybody left happy, but a little wet.

Reading activities

Past simple + past continuous together 10 min

We often use both tenses together: a long action in progress (past continuous) and a short action that interrupts it (past simple).

Example
+I was doing my homework when the lights went out.
long action (continuous) + when + short action (simple)
+While I was reading, my sister was watching TV.
while + two actions happening at the same time (both continuous)
?What were you doing when the storm started?

When

  • Use when + past simple for the short action that interrupts.
  • I was walking home when it started to rain.

While

  • Use while + past continuous for two actions in progress at the same time.
  • While I was studying, my brother was cooking.

Look back at the model paragraph in the Writing tab: "I was doing my homework when the lights went out." That's exactly this pattern!

Practice

An unusual or busy moment 10 min

Writing a scene description

Step 1: Plan your scene

Think of an unusual or busy moment you remember (a storm, a trip delay, a busy day, a blackout). Write five people or things and what they were doing.

Step 2: Write your paragraph

Write a paragraph of 60–80 words describing that moment. Use the past continuous, and the past simple if you need it for the main events.

Useful language

  • At [time], I was… · Meanwhile, … was/were …ing.
  • Suddenly, … · In the background, …
  • Nobody was … · Everybody was …
0 words (aim for 60–80)
